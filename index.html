Level Type

Thirty to forty-five minutes a day beats a four-hour Sunday every single time. Pick the level that feels slightly too easy and start there — comprehension, not struggle, is what builds a language.

Getting German media onto your machine

I've deliberately linked to sources rather than bundling copies of films and news articles into this file — most of that material is copyrighted, and a page full of scraped media would break within weeks anyway. What follows is the stuff that is genuinely, legally downloadable, plus the two tools that do the most work.

Public-domain books (EPUB / txt, yours forever)

  • Project Gutenberg — German bookshelf — thousands of titles, EPUB and plain text, no account. Goethe, Kafka, Fontane, the Grimms.
  • Projekt Gutenberg-DE — the German-hosted sibling, broader German-language catalogue.
  • Zeno.org — big free German literature and reference archive.

Public-domain audiobooks (MP3)

  • LibriVox — German recordings — volunteer-read, free MP3 download or podcast feed. Pair a LibriVox recording with the matching Gutenberg text and you have a parallel audiobook for nothing.
  • Ohrka — free, professionally produced children's audiobooks. Genuinely the best-kept secret for A2–B1 listening.

Podcasts — subscribe once, they download themselves

Every podcast in the Library tab has an RSS feed. Add them in Apple Podcasts, Pocket Casts, AntennaPod or Overcast and set "download new episodes automatically" — that is your commute solved permanently. The highest-value three:

  • Langsam gesprochene Nachrichten (DW) — the day's real news, read slowly. B1–B2.
  • Slow German mit Annik Rubens — short cultural monologues with transcripts. A2–B1.
  • Easy German — two natives talking naturally, transcripts behind a cheap membership. B1+.

The two tools that matter most

  • Language Reactor — a browser extension that puts dual German + English subtitles on Netflix and YouTube, lets you click any word for a definition, and replays lines. This single extension turns your existing streaming subscription into a full learning platform. Start here.
  • Anki — spaced repetition flashcards, free on desktop and Android. Make cards only from words you met in something you actually read or watched; downloaded 5,000-word decks get abandoned.

Offline in the apps themselves

  • DW Learn German app — the whole Nicos Weg A1–B1 course downloads for offline use, free, no ads.
  • ARD Audiothek and ZDFmediathek apps — download episodes for offline playback. Some ARD/ZDF video is geo-restricted to Germany; the audio generally is not.
  • Spotify / YouTube Premium — offline downloads for German music and channels if you already pay for them.
One honest warning about geo-blocking. ARD and ZDF Mediathek restrict a good chunk of their video library to German IP addresses for licensing reasons. Their news and children's programming — Tagesschau, logo!, Sendung mit der Maus, Löwenzahn — is mostly available worldwide, and that happens to be exactly the material a learner wants. Start there and you'll rarely hit a wall.
